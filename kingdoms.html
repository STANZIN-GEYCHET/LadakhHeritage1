The beginning

The Kingdom
of Maryul

The story of Ladakh's kingdom begins with Maryul, an early realm that emerged after the Tibetan Empire fragmented. Around the 10th century, Kyide Nyimagon's territories were divided among his descendants. His son Lhachen Palgyigon is traditionally associated with establishing rule over Maryul, the region that became the heartland of Ladakh.

Over the centuries, Maryul developed a distinct political identity in the high mountains. Its communities and rulers were connected to neighbouring Himalayan and Tibetan regions through trade, religion, and travel. The early chronology is not completely certain, but Maryul is an important starting point in the recorded history of Ladakh's kingdom.

02 / THE NEXT DYNASTY c. 15th century–1842
The rise of a dynasty

The Namgyal
Dynasty

Around the 15th century, Bhagan—also known as Lhachen Bhagan in some historical accounts—rose to power after challenging the rulers of Maryul. Through an alliance with the people of Leh, he established a new dynasty that became known as Namgyal, a name commonly understood to mean “victorious.”

The dynasty shaped Ladakh's political and cultural history for centuries. One of its best-known rulers was Sengge Namgyal, remembered as the “Lion King,” who supported important royal and monastic building projects. The dynasty's era ended in 1842, when Dogra forces conquered Ladakh, bringing the period of independent Ladakhi kingship to a close.

A separate timeline

Through
the Ages

A few milestones help trace the broad arc of Ladakh's royal history. Early dates are approximate, as historical records do not establish every detail with certainty.

10th century

Maryul takes shape

After the fragmentation of the Tibetan Empire, regional rule developed under descendants of Kyide Nyimagon. Lhachen Palgyigon is traditionally linked with Maryul.

c. 1460

The Namgyal dynasty begins

Bhagan rose to power and established a new ruling dynasty, marking a major turning point in Ladakh's royal history.

17th century

The age of Sengge Namgyal

Sengge Namgyal became one of Ladakh's most celebrated rulers, associated with ambitious building projects and a flourishing royal legacy.

1842

The end of independent kingship

Dogra conquest brought the era of independent Ladakhi kingship to an end and reshaped the region's political future.
